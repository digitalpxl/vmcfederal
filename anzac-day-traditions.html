Anzac Day is a sacred occasion for Australians and New Zealanders, commemorating the bravery, sacrifice and mateship of those who have served. For members of the Veterans Motorcycle Club it holds deep significance — a chance to honour fallen comrades, reconnect with fellow veterans and reflect on the enduring spirit of the Anzacs. One of the day's long-standing traditions is the Gunfire Breakfast, a ritual with historical, social and symbolic meaning for veterans and their communities.

The historical significance

The term "Gunfire Breakfast" dates back to the First World War, when soldiers received a simple pre-dawn meal before heading into battle. It was a moment of camaraderie before the uncertainty of the battlefield.

The tradition has carried on over the decades, symbolising both remembrance and resilience. For VMC members, sharing a Gunfire Breakfast after the Dawn Service honours that legacy — paying tribute to past generations while strengthening the bonds between veterans today.

A time for reflection and camaraderie

The Veterans Motorcycle Club is more than a group of riders; it's a brotherhood of people who have served their country and continue to support one another. The Gunfire Breakfast gives veterans a place to gather, share stories and offer mutual support.

Many veterans face challenges such as PTSD, social isolation and the transition to civilian life. Coming together over a meal on Anzac Day builds a sense of belonging and reminds members they're never alone. Sitting down for a warm breakfast with people who understand the journey is an invaluable part of healing.

Honouring tradition and mateship

For the VMC, Anzac Day is about more than attending services — it's about actively keeping the Anzac spirit alive. Whether it's raising a toast to fallen comrades, sharing memories of service or simply enjoying the company of old and new friends, the Gunfire Breakfast reinforces the deep connections between veterans.

Mateship — a core value of the military and veteran community.

The VMC's role in Anzac Day

Each year, VMC chapters across Australia take part in local Anzac Day events, riding in solidarity to dawn services, parades and memorials. The Gunfire Breakfast is often hosted at local RSL clubs, community halls or VMC clubhouses, where members and their families share in the tradition.

Many members also support Anzac Day events by providing escorts, assisting with flag-raising ceremonies or helping older veterans attend services. Their presence is a powerful reminder that the Anzac spirit lives on in those who continue to serve their communities.

Keeping it alive for future generations

As the years pass, it's essential that the stories and traditions of Anzac Day are passed on. The Gunfire Breakfast is a chance to educate and inspire younger veterans, families and community members about what the day means. By keeping these traditions, the VMC helps preserve the legacy of service, sacrifice and remembrance.

More than a meal

For the Veterans Motorcycle Club, the Gunfire Breakfast is a symbol of remembrance, unity and the enduring Anzac spirit. As the sun rises on Anzac Day, veterans gather in the cool morning air, share a drink in honour of fallen comrades and reinforce the bonds of mateship that have carried them through. It's a testament to the strength and resilience of those who served — and a promise that the legacy of the Anzacs will never be forgotten.
