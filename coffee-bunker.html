Why we exist

Life after service looks different. Connection still matters.

Whether you left Defence last month or years ago, finding your people again can make a difference.

The Coffee Bunker is a veteran-led catch-up for people who've served and want somewhere real to connect — without appointments, labels or pressure. For some it's about rebuilding the sense of team that came with service. For others it's simply a place to meet people outside work, family and the civilian routine.

  • You don't need to be struggling.
  • You don't need to tell your story.
  • You don't need to ride.
  • Just turn up and grab a coffee.

Veterans supporting veterans

Support should feel human.

For the Federal Chapter, veteran support means being present, staying connected and acting when someone needs a hand.

That might be checking in after a rough week, helping someone with the next step after Defence, introducing a new veteran to the group — or just giving someone a reason to get out of the house.

You leave Defence. You don't leave the people behind.
Small actions matter
  • Checking in when someone drops off the radar.
  • Making the call, sending the message and following through.
  • Helping a veteran or family with practical problems.
  • Creating reasons to get out, ride, talk and reconnect.
  • Backing people through transition, work, family and life after service.
  • Helping people find professional support when it's needed.

2021Where it started

A simple idea with an important purpose.

The Coffee Bunker started with a simple idea: give veterans somewhere informal to meet, have a coffee and reconnect outside the traditional support-system environment.

Today that idea matters just as much for contemporary veterans balancing civilian careers, study, young families, reserve service, transition, mental health, identity and the loss of the close-knit teams they once worked beside.

Success isn't measured in attendance numbers. If someone walks away with a new contact, a new riding mate, a useful conversation or simply the feeling they belong somewhere, the morning has done its job.
