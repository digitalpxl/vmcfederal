Australia's battle honours

Across more than a century, Australians have fought, served, protected, rebuilt and kept the peace in some of the hardest places in the world. Behind every honour are the people who served — those who came home, those who carried the effects of service for life, and those who never came home.

1914–1918First World War

The First World War

Thousands of Australians volunteered for the Australian Imperial Force, serving at Gallipoli, on the Western Front and throughout the Middle East in a conflict that left an enduring mark on the nation.

Gallipoli

On 25 April 1915, Australian and New Zealand troops landed on the Gallipoli peninsula. The campaign continued for eight months under extraordinary hardship. Though it didn't achieve the result the Allies sought, the courage, endurance and mateship shown there became part of Australia's national story.

8,709Australians died

The Western Front

From 1916, Australian divisions fought in France and Belgium. Fromelles, Pozières, Bullecourt, Messines, Passchendaele, Villers-Bretonneux, Hamel and Mont St Quentin became names permanently linked to Australian service and sacrifice.

60,000+Australian lives lost in WWI, from a population under five million
1939–1945Second World War

The Second World War

Australians served across Europe, North Africa, the Mediterranean, the Middle East, South-East Asia and the Pacific — in Greece and Crete, Tobruk and El Alamein, Malaya and Singapore, Milne Bay, Kokoda and the wider New Guinea and Pacific campaigns.

Tobruk

In 1941, Australian troops formed a major part of the Allied garrison defending the Libyan port of Tobruk, enduring months of siege, artillery fire and repeated attacks. They became famously known as the Rats of Tobruk.

Kokoda

In 1942, Australian and Papuan forces fought to stop the Japanese advance across the Owen Stanley Range towards Port Moresby, through mountains, jungle, mud, disease and exhaustion. The courage and support of Papuan soldiers and carriers were critical.

600+Australians killed · more than 1,000 wounded

1950–1953Korean War

Korea

Australia joined the United Nations effort in Korea after war broke out in 1950. Army, Navy and Air Force personnel served throughout.

17,000+served · 340 killed · more than 1,200 wounded

Kapyong

In April 1951, soldiers of the 3rd Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment helped hold the Kapyong Valley during a major Chinese offensive, stopping a breakthrough towards Seoul.

Kapyong remains one of the most significant battle honours carried by the Royal Australian Regiment.

1948–19661948–60 · 1963–66

Malaya & Indonesian Confrontation

Australian forces served in South-East Asia during the Malayan Emergency and later Indonesian Confrontation, supporting Commonwealth and Malaysian forces through jungle operations, maritime patrols, aviation and regional security. Much of this service happened far from public attention, but it remains an important chapter in Australia's post-war history.

1962–1972Vietnam War

Vietnam

Australians served in Vietnam in combat, training, aviation, naval, medical and support roles.

60,000+Australians served

Long Tan

On 18 August 1966, D Company, 6th Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment fought a major action in a rubber plantation near Long Tan in Phuoc Tuy Province. Heavily outnumbered and fighting through torrential rain, they held their position with artillery support until a relief force arrived.

18Australians killed · 24 wounded

Coral & Balmoral

Between May and June 1968, Australian forces fought a series of major engagements around Fire Support Bases Coral and Balmoral, north-east of Saigon — some of the largest and most sustained combat Australians experienced in Vietnam.

From 1976Modern ADF

The modern Australian Defence Force

The ADF was formed in 1976, bringing the Navy, Army and Air Force together under one defence organisation. Since then, Australians have served in combat, peacekeeping, regional security, humanitarian emergencies and disaster response.

1990–1993Gulf · Somalia

The Gulf War & Somalia

The Gulf War · 1990–91

Australia deployed naval forces to the Middle East after Iraq invaded Kuwait. Australian ships conducted interception, escort, surveillance and support operations in the Persian Gulf.

Somalia · 1992–93

Under Operation Solace, Australian soldiers helped establish security around Baidoa so humanitarian aid could reach communities affected by conflict and famine.

From 1999East Timor · Timor-Leste

East Timor & Timor-Leste

In 1999 Australia led the International Force East Timor (INTERFET) after violence surrounding East Timor's independence vote. Thousands of Australians served to restore security and support the East Timorese people, later returning during periods of instability and continuing to support the developing nation of Timor-Leste.

2001–2021Afghanistan

Afghanistan

Australian forces served in special operations, reconstruction, mentoring and training, aviation, force protection, logistics and coalition support. Many more returned home carrying physical and psychological injuries.

41ADF personnel died on operations

We remember those who served, those who were wounded and those who never returned.

2003 onwardsIraq · Syria

Iraq & the fight against Islamic State

Australian forces took part in coalition operations in Iraq from 2003, and later returned to the Middle East as part of the international campaign against Islamic State.

Operation Okra

Australian personnel contributed air power, training, command, intelligence and support capabilities to coalition operations in Iraq and Syria.

Since 1947Peacekeeping

Peacekeeping

Soldiers, sailors, aviators, police and civilians have observed ceasefires, protected communities, supported elections, delivered aid and helped rebuild nations — in Indonesia, Kashmir, Cyprus, Cambodia, Rwanda, the Middle East, Bougainville, Timor-Leste, Solomon Islands, Sudan and South Sudan.

Peacekeeping differs from conventional warfare, but it demands the same courage, discipline, restraint, professionalism and commitment to others.

OngoingHumanitarian service

Humanitarian service

Across Australia and the Indo-Pacific, ADF personnel provide evacuation, engineering, medical, aviation, maritime, transport and logistics support after cyclones, floods, bushfires, earthquakes, tsunamis and other disasters.

Carrying a rifle, flying an aircraft, sailing a ship, rebuilding a road or helping a community recover — service remains service.

More than names on a wall

To veterans and their families, they represent people.

Young Australians who left their homes and families. People who relied on their mates in circumstances few of us will ever face. Generations who accepted the responsibility of service.

Some came home to parades. Others returned quietly. Some carried injuries that could be seen; others carried wounds that could not. And some never came home. That is why we remember.

Their legacy continues

Every generation inherits a legacy built by those who served before — not only battles won or medals awarded, but courage when things are hard, endurance when the road is long, mateship when someone needs support, and sacrifice when duty demands more than words.

For veterans, remembrance is personal: the names we recognise, the stories we tell, the mates we still think about and the empty places left by those who are gone.

  • We honour all who served.
  • We remember those who never returned.
  • We stand beside those who still carry the cost of service.

We will remember them.
